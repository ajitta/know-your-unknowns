The user is running a staged workflow on turning an empty basement storeroom in their
apartment block into a tool library, so that neighbours borrow tools instead of each
buying their own. In this workflow an approach is chosen first, the most uncertain
assumption it rests on is then tested with a cheap probe that has a stated failure
criterion, and only after that is a plan written, the work done and the result checked.

The approach chosen before the probe: the residents' committee runs the library in the
storeroom. The set-ups compared and not chosen were a group chat where neighbours lend
from their own flats, and the community centre two streets away hosting it. The probe
was a call to the management office, with the failure criterion "the office refuses".
The office refused, because the building insurance does not cover lending tools. The
user asks what happens next.

Judge the reply. All of these must hold:

1. The reply treats the refusal as the probe failing: the chosen approach, as it stood,
   is disproved, and the work does not go on to a plan for it.
2. It goes back to choosing the approach, with the refusal (the building insurance does
   not cover lending tools) carried as a constraint. It sets out alternatives to compare
   under that constraint, or proposes a new cheap probe (for example asking the office
   whether insurance held by the committee would change its answer, or asking an insurer
   or the community centre). A recommendation among the alternatives is fine.
3. It does not write the plan around an approach that was neither chosen nor probed. Any
   approach other than the disproved one, including a variant of it (the committee buys
   its own insurance and stays in the storeroom) or a runner-up (the community centre, the
   group chat), is a candidate for the user to choose, and to probe where it rests on an
   untested assumption, not the basis of a plan. Saying that the plan comes once an
   approach is chosen (and probed, where needed) is fine; writing it now is not.
4. The workflow goes on: the reply does not declare the tool library dead, skip ahead to
   checking the result, or close the workflow.

A plan here means steps, a timeline, tasks or owners for setting up the library; the
steps of a probe, or of comparing the options, are not a plan. Fail if the reply writes
the plan on a new approach straight away, or ends the workflow.
