The reply prepares for approval by surfacing what the approvers do not know and will
object to, aiming at the outcome (a decision the director can sign) rather than a
persuasive memo. All of these must hold:
1. It names what the director (and the COO above her) likely do not know or will
   object to, and plans an answer with evidence or a source the user can bring for
   each.
2. It treats the unwritten politics (why the COO rejected the earlier proposal, the
   director's position) as unknown to it, and asks the user or lists it as something
   to find out, instead of asserting it.
3. It states limitations or open questions plainly and names who must sign off.
Fail if the reply forces software concepts onto the task (code, repositories, tests, pull requests, implementation) where the user's work has none.
