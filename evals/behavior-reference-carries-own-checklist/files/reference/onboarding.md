# New Volunteer Onboarding Guide: Northgate Branch

Kestrel Valley Libraries, Northgate Branch. Kept by the Volunteer Coordinator, Priya Raman.
Last revised February 2026.

Welcome, and thank you for giving your time. About 40 volunteers keep Northgate's shelves in
order, run the Saturday homework club, set up events and take books to housebound readers.
This guide walks you from your application to your first shift.

## 1. Who can volunteer

- You must be 16 or older. Young people aged 14 or 15 may help with the summer reading
  challenge only, with a parent's signed consent.
- We ask for one three-hour shift a week for at least three months, so the people you help
  see a familiar face.

## 2. Applying

- Fill in the volunteer application at the front desk or on the library website, and name
  two referees who are not family.
- Tell us which roles interest you. The homework club, story time and the home-delivery
  service involve children or visits to patrons' homes, so they need an enhanced background
  check. The library pays for it; it usually takes two to three weeks.
- The coordinator will call you within a week to talk about roles and shift times.

## 3. Orientation

Orientation is a 90-minute group session on the first Tuesday of each month, 6:00 to
7:30 pm, in the Community Room. It covers:

- what the library is for and how the branch is run;
- patron privacy: volunteers never look up, read out or talk about what anyone borrows;
- a tour of the building, the fire exits and the assembly point (the far end of the car
  park, by the bus shelter), and where the first-aid kit is kept;
- signing in and out, and what to do if you cannot make a shift.

At the end you sign the volunteer agreement and the patron-privacy pledge, and hand in your
emergency contact form.

## 4. Your shadow session

Before your first shift you spend two hours alongside a buddy, an experienced volunteer the
coordinator pairs you with. You watch, then try the tasks with your buddy beside you.
Afterwards your buddy sends the coordinator a short note to say you are ready.

## 5. Shifts and signing in

- The rota goes out by email on the 20th of each month for the month after.
- Sign in and out on the clipboard at the staff entrance every time. The duty manager takes
  it outside if the fire alarm sounds, so it doubles as our fire roll.
- To swap or drop a shift, email the coordinator at least 48 hours ahead. If you are ill on
  the day, phone the front desk.

## 6. What volunteers do and do not do

Volunteers shelve and shelf-read, help at the homework club, set up and clear away events,
and deliver books to housebound readers (always in pairs).

Volunteers do not handle cash, issue or renew library cards, open patron accounts on the
computer, or stay alone with a child. If a patron asks for any of these, fetch a member of
staff.

## 7. Who to contact

- Volunteer Coordinator: Priya Raman, Monday to Thursday, extension 214.
- At other times, the duty manager on the front desk.

## Onboarding checklist (signed off before the first shift)

The coordinator ticks each item and signs the form. Nobody works a shift until all five are
ticked.

1. Volunteer agreement and patron-privacy pledge signed and filed.
2. Two references received and, for roles with children or home visits, the enhanced
   background check cleared.
3. Orientation attended, including the building tour and the fire assembly point.
4. Shadow session completed, and the buddy's note returned to the coordinator.
5. Emergency contact form on file, and the volunteer added to the shift rota.
