The reply runs a blind-spot pass on a grant application, aiming at the outcome (a
fundable application) rather than producing a draft. All of these must hold:
1. It is either the opening question -- asking the user what they are sure of, what they
   are assuming and where they cannot judge -- or a full pass whose findings name
   specific risks of this kind of grant (eligibility, the library's or city's rules,
   permissions, budget rules, reporting duties) with why each bites.
2. It separates what it does not know about the user's situation or cannot check from
   here (the actual call text, the city's rules, the libraries' consent) from what it
   states as fact, by labelling, asking, or listing it as something to check.
3. It does not write the grant application itself.
Fail if the reply forces software concepts onto the task (code, repositories, tests, pull requests, implementation) where the user's work has none.
